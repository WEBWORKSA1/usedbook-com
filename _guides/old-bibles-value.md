Old Bibles are among the books people most often ask about. They're frequently large, impressive, leather-bound and more than a century old, so it's natural to expect them to be valuable. The honest answer is that most aren't worth a great deal on the market, but some are, and nearly all carry important family history.

## Why most old Bibles have modest value

The Bible has been one of the most printed books in history. In the 19th century, large illustrated family Bibles were sold widely, often by subscription, to households that kept them for generations. Because so many were made and so many survive, supply is high. Many are also worn, with loose bindings, detached covers or damp damage. As a result, typical 19th-century family Bibles tend to sell for relatively modest sums, and some are hard to sell at all.

## What can make a Bible valuable

- **Age and importance of the edition.** Early printings, particularly from the first centuries of printing, landmark translations and important early editions printed in a particular country or language attract collectors.
- **Early regional imprints.** Bibles printed early in a region's printing history can be significant to collectors and institutions.
- **Fine bindings.** Exceptional decorative bindings by notable binders, in good condition.
- **Illustrations.** Editions with notable engravings or artists, complete and in good condition.
- **Provenance.** Ownership by a notable person or family, or a documented historical connection.
- **Rarity and condition.** As with any book, scarce editions in good, complete condition are worth more.

If your Bible may fall into one of these categories, research the exact edition (title page, printer, place, date) and seek an [expert opinion](/get-offers/#valuation).

## The real treasure: family records

Many family Bibles contain pages recording births, marriages and deaths, sometimes going back generations, along with pressed flowers, letters, photographs and newspaper clippings. These can be invaluable for genealogy.

- **Photograph or scan** every family record page in good light.
- **Transcribe** the entries and share them with relatives.
- **Keep any loose items** together with notes on where you found them.
- **Consider archives:** genealogical societies and historical societies may welcome copies of the records, and some accept family Bibles themselves.

## Caring for an old Bible

Large old Bibles are heavy, and their bindings often struggle. Store them flat, in a cool, dry place out of direct light. Support the covers when opening, and don't force the book open flat. Avoid tape and household glue. For a fragile family Bible you want to keep, a professional book conservator or binder can stabilise or rebind it. Read our [book care guide](/guides/how-to-store-and-clean-books/) and [repair guide](/guides/basic-book-repair/).

## Selling or rehoming an old Bible

1. Identify the edition and record its condition honestly.
2. Preserve the family records first.
3. Check comparable sold examples, not asking prices.
4. For potentially important editions, consult a rare-book dealer or auction house.
5. For typical family Bibles, consider offering it to relatives, a local historical society or church, or selling it as a decorative or genealogical item.

Whatever its market value, an old family Bible is often one of the most meaningful books a family owns. Treat its records with care.
