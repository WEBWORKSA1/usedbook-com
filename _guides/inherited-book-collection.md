    a: It varies enormously. Most collections are worth relatively little per book, but many contain a small number of books worth far more than the rest combined. Identifying those is the key to getting fair value.
  - q: Should I get an appraisal?
    a: You need a formal appraisal for insurance, estate settlement, tax purposes or a charitable donation claim in some jurisdictions. To decide whether to sell, an informal review by a reputable dealer or service is usually enough.

    a: Yes. Bulk buyers, estate buyers and some booksellers will make an offer for an entire collection and remove it. Separating likely valuable books first, or getting the whole collection reviewed, usually leads to a better outcome.

Clearing a loved one's books is often emotional and rushed. Shelves need emptying, a house needs selling, and it's tempting to call the first person who will take everything away. A few hours of careful sorting first can protect a meaningful amount of value, and make sure books important to the family aren't lost. Here's a calm plan.

## Step 1: Pause and protect

- **Don't throw anything away yet,** including papers tucked inside books.
- **Don't let anyone take "just a few"** before the collection has been looked at.
- **Keep books where they are** if the space is dry. If they're in a damp basement or garage, move them somewhere dry first.
- **Check inside books** for letters, photographs, money, bookmarks and notes. Families often find personal treasures this way.

## Step 2: Get a quick picture

Photograph each bookcase so the spines are readable. Note rough numbers: how many books, how many shelves or boxes. Our [Collection Estimator](/tools/collection-estimator/) turns rough counts by type into a ballpark figure.

## Step 3: Pull out likely high-value books

You don't need to research every book. Instead, look for categories that tend to carry value:

- **Old books**, especially before 1900, and anything with leather bindings or hand-coloured plates
- **First editions in dust jackets** of well-known 20th-century authors
- **Signed or inscribed** books
- **Limited and fine-press editions**, often numbered, in slipcases
- **Art, photography and design books**, particularly large and out-of-print ones
- **Local history, maps, atlases and travel** from earlier eras
- **Specialist collections:** natural history, science, medicine, military history, sport, cookery
- **Children's books** from the early to mid 20th century in good condition
- **Anything the owner clearly treasured:** kept in a separate case, wrapped, or catalogued

Put these to one side. Then check a few with our [Book Value Estimator](/tools/book-value/) and [First-Edition Checker](/tools/first-edition-checker/).

## Step 4: Decide what the family keeps

Invite family members to choose books with personal meaning before anything is sold. Family Bibles, annotated favourites and books with inscriptions often matter more than money. (Old family Bibles have their own considerations; see our [Bible guide](/guides/old-bibles-value/).)

## Step 5: Get informed offers

Depending on size and content, options include:

- **A collection review and offers.** Describe the books and share photos through our [Get Offers](/get-offers/) service; we reply with practical next steps and, where possible, offers from suitable buyers.
- **Local used or antiquarian booksellers**, who may visit for larger collections.
- **Auction houses** with book departments, for significant items.
- **Selling the best books individually** online, if someone has the time.

Get more than one opinion for anything that looks valuable, and be wary of anyone pressing for an immediate decision.

## Step 6: Formal appraisal, if you need one

If you need a valuation for estate settlement, insurance, tax or a charitable donation claim, hire a qualified appraiser who specialises in books. Appraisals are paid professional services and should be independent of anyone offering to buy.

## Step 7: Donate and recycle the rest

Once valuable and personal books are dealt with, the remaining books can go to libraries, Friends groups, charity shops, schools or book drives. Damaged and unwanted reference books can be recycled. See [where to donate used books](/guides/where-to-donate-used-books/).

## A simple timeline

1. **Day 1:** Protect the books, check inside them, photograph the shelves.
2. **Days 2-3:** Pull out likely valuable categories; family chooses keepsakes.
3. **Week 1:** Request reviews and offers; research standout books.
4. **Week 2+:** Sell, appraise if needed, then donate and recycle the remainder.

Taking these steps in order means nothing important is lost, and you'll make decisions with real information rather than under pressure.
