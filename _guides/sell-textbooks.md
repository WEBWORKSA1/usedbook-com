Textbooks are the one category of used book that reliably sells for real money, which is why there's a whole industry of buyback services competing for them. But textbook value is fragile: it depends on the edition, the time of year and the condition. Here's how to get the best return.

## Sell fast: timing beats everything

Textbook prices follow the academic calendar. Demand rises before each term as students buy, and buyback quotes are often strongest in the weeks before classes start. Value then falls whenever a **new edition** is announced, sometimes to almost nothing overnight. The single best tactic is simply to sell as soon as you no longer need the book.

## Check the edition and ISBN

Buyback services quote by ISBN, so enter the exact number from the back cover or copyright page. Watch for:

- **Newer editions.** If a newer edition exists, quotes for yours will be low or nil.
- **International editions.** Often marked "Not for sale in the USA" or similar. Many buyback services won't accept them.
- **Instructor's or examination copies.** Usually marked as not for resale and generally not accepted.
- **Custom editions** produced for one school or course are hard to sell elsewhere.
- **Loose-leaf versions** and packages with access codes have their own rules.

## Compare buyback offers

Quotes for the same ISBN can vary widely between buyers. Comparison services collect offers from many vendors at once. Enter your ISBN in our [sell tool](/sell/) to open them, and check:

- The quote for your condition (some quote separately for "like new" and "good")
- Minimum order value for free shipping
- How and when you get paid
- The service's condition rules for highlighting and water damage

Splitting books between two or three buyers to take the best quote for each can add up, as long as you meet each one's minimum.

## Condition rules that matter

Buyback services inspect books on arrival. Common reasons for rejection or reduced payment include heavy highlighting or writing, water damage, missing pages, broken bindings, missing CDs or supplements that were part of the package, and strong odours. Light highlighting is often accepted, but rules differ by service. Grade honestly with our [Condition Grader](/tools/condition-grader/).

## Selling directly to other students

You can often get more by selling directly through campus groups, noticeboards or marketplaces, because you cut out the middleman. The trade-off is time and uncertainty. A reasonable approach: get a buyback quote first, list it yourself slightly above that for a week or two, and fall back to the buyback if it doesn't sell, as long as the quote hasn't dropped.

## What to do with textbooks nobody will buy

Outdated editions still have uses. Some charities ship textbooks to schools and libraries that can use them; some campuses run book drives; and many recycling programmes accept books with hard covers removed. See [where to donate used books](/guides/where-to-donate-used-books/).

## Textbook selling checklist

1. Sell as soon as the course ends.
2. Use the exact ISBN and check for newer editions.
3. Compare at least two or three buyback quotes.
4. Read each buyer's condition rules before shipping.
5. Bundle books to clear minimum order values.
6. Keep tracking numbers and photos until you're paid.
