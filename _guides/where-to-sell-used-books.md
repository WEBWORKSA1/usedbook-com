There are more places to sell used books than ever, and each one rewards a different kind of book. Choosing well is the difference between a quick, fair sale and a box of rejections. This guide compares the main options and explains when each makes sense.

## Instant buyback services

**How it works:** you enter an ISBN, receive an instant quote, ship the book with a prepaid label and get paid once it is checked.

**Best for:** current textbooks, recent professional and technical books, and popular nonfiction with steady demand.

**Pros:** fast, predictable, little effort, free shipping above a minimum order.

**Cons:** many books are declined; quotes are below what you might get listing the book yourself; condition is checked strictly.

Comparison sites gather quotes from several buyback vendors so you do not have to check each one. Start with our [sell tool](/sell/), which links your ISBN to these services.

## Online marketplaces

**How it works:** you list the book, set a price and ship when it sells. Marketplaces include Amazon, eBay, AbeBooks, Biblio and Alibris.

**Best for:** specialist and out-of-print nonfiction, collectibles, signed books and anything with a defined audience.

**Pros:** access to buyers worldwide; usually the highest prices for desirable books.

**Cons:** fees, listing time, storage, packing, customer service and the risk of returns. Some books sit for months.

Each marketplace has a character. AbeBooks and Biblio attract collectors and readers looking for specific editions; eBay suits auctions, lots and collectibles and is the best place to research sold prices; Amazon moves high volumes of in-demand titles but is competitive on price.

## Peer-to-peer apps and local classifieds

Apps that let readers sell to each other, along with local listing sites and community groups, work well for popular fiction, series sets and children's books. Selling locally avoids postage, which is often the biggest cost on low-value books. Use common sense with meetups and payment.

## Local used bookstores

**Best for:** clean, popular stock and readers who will happily take store credit.

Used bookstores buy what they can sell from their own shelves. Expect to be offered a fraction of the shelf price, often more in credit than in cash. Call before you go: many stores buy only on certain days, only in certain subjects, or not at all when they are full. Bring books in good condition, with anything special separated so the buyer can see it.

## Specialist dealers and auction houses

For rare and antiquarian books, signed and association copies, fine bindings, early printings, maps and manuscripts, a specialist dealer or auction house is often the right buyer. Dealers buy outright and take the risk; auctions can achieve strong prices for important items but charge commission and take time. Members of professional associations such as the ABAA in the United States and the ILAB worldwide subscribe to codes of ethics, which is reassuring when selling something valuable.

Before you approach a dealer, identify the edition and condition yourself using our [value estimator](/tools/book-value/) and [first-edition checker](/tools/first-edition-checker/). Informed sellers get better conversations.

## Bulk buyers and collection offers

If you have hundreds or thousands of books, selling them one by one may not be realistic. Bulk buyers, estate buyers and some used bookstores will make an offer for an entire collection. Per-book prices are low because the buyer takes on sorting, storage and unsold stock. The way to raise a bulk offer is to identify and separate the valuable books first. ## Book fairs and pop-up stalls

If you have collectible stock and enjoy meeting buyers, local book fairs, flea markets and antiques markets can work, especially for illustrated books, vintage paperbacks and local history. Factor in table fees and time.

## Quick comparison

| Option | Effort | Speed | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyback sites | Low | Fast | Low-fair | Textbooks, in-demand ISBNs |
| Marketplaces | High | Slow-medium | Highest | Specialist, collectible |
| Peer-to-peer and local | Medium | Medium | Fair | Popular fiction, sets |
| Used bookstore | Low | Same day | Low (credit higher) | Clean general stock |
| Dealer or auction | Medium | Medium | Strong for rare books | Rare, signed, antiquarian |
| Bulk buyer | Very low | Fast | Low per book | Large mixed collections |

## What usually won't sell

Be realistic about books with little or no resale market: older encyclopedia sets, outdated textbooks and computer manuals, condensed books, common book-club editions, mass-market bestsellers from past decades, and damaged or musty copies. These are better [donated, swapped or recycled](/guides/where-to-donate-used-books/).

## The bottom line

Match the book to the route. Run in-demand ISBNs through buyback comparisons, list specialist and collectible books yourself or through a dealer, and move the rest in bulk or by donation. And never sell a collection by the box until someone has looked for the few books that make up most of its value.
