Book scouting is the craft of finding used books that are worth more than you pay for them, and reselling them at a profit. It ranges from a weekend hobby to full-time businesses moving thousands of books a month. The fundamentals are the same at every scale.

## Where scouts find books

- **Library book sales** run by Friends of the Library groups, especially preview nights and bag days
- **Thrift and charity stores**, which restock frequently
- **Estate sales and auctions**, where whole libraries appear at once
- **Garage and yard sales**, best for specific niches
- **Church, school and community fundraisers**
- **Used bookstores' bargain shelves**, which sometimes hold specialist books priced as general stock
- **Buying collections directly** from people who are moving or downsizing

## What tends to sell

Scouts learn categories that repeatedly earn a profit:

- **Specialist nonfiction:** medicine, law, engineering, computing (current), accounting, nursing and other professional subjects
- **Hobby and craft:** knitting, woodworking, model railways, fishing, gardening, cookery from respected authors
- **Academic and university press books**, especially in history, philosophy and theology
- **Textbooks** in current editions
- **Out-of-print titles** with a following, including some older self-help, spirituality and technical books
- **Collectible fiction:** first printings with jackets of authors in demand
- **Art, photography and design books**, particularly large-format and out-of-print
- **Children's books** with lasting demand and collectible illustrated editions

Generally, avoid mass-market paperbacks, recent bestsellers, book-club editions, old encyclopedias and damaged copies unless you know a specific exception.

## Checking a book on the spot

The core question at the shelf is simple: *will this sell, how soon, and for how much after costs?*

1. **Scan or search the ISBN** to see current prices and how often it sells. Most scouting apps show a sales-rank or demand indicator.
2. **Check the competition**: how many copies are listed, and at what price for your condition?
3. **Estimate net profit**: expected sale price, minus fees, postage, packaging and what you'll pay. Our [Reseller Profit Calculator](/tools/reseller-profit-calculator/) shows margin, ROI and break-even.
4. **Check condition**: highlighting, water damage and missing pieces kill margins.

Many scouts set a minimum profit per book and walk away from anything below it, no matter how tempting the price.

## Books without barcodes

Older books with no ISBN won't scan. Here, knowledge wins. Learn which publishers, subjects and eras sell, how to spot a [first edition](/guides/how-to-identify-a-first-edition/) and a [book club edition](/guides/book-club-editions/), and which features add value: dust jackets, signatures, illustrations, maps. Look up promising finds by title on sold listings.

## Building a simple operation

- **Storage:** dry, cool and organised, with an inventory system so you can find each book when it sells.
- **Listing:** accurate titles, ISBNs, honest grades and specific condition notes. Use our [Condition Grader](/tools/condition-grader/) for consistent notes.
- **Shipping:** stock rigid mailers and boxes in common sizes. See [how to ship books](/guides/how-to-ship-books/).
- **Records:** track cost, sale price, fees and postage per book so you know which sources and categories really pay.

## Mistakes that sink new scouts

- **Buying volume instead of value.** A garage full of slow sellers is a cost, not an asset.
- **Ignoring postage.** Heavy books can eat the whole margin.
- **Over-grading.** Returns and complaints are expensive.
- **Chasing low-priced competition.** Pricing races to the bottom on common books leave nothing for you.
- **Not tracking numbers.** Without records, you can't tell good sources from bad ones.

## The scout's mindset

The best scouts are patient, curious and disciplined. They know their categories, they pass on most books, and they keep learning: every unexpected sale teaches something new. Start small, track everything, and let the numbers guide you.
