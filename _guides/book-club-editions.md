For much of the 20th century, subscription book clubs sold millions of hardcovers to members by mail. Many of those books still circulate today, and they cause more disappointment than almost anything else in book collecting. A book club edition (BCE) can look almost identical to the trade first edition on the shelf, yet be worth a small fraction of it. Here's how to tell them apart.

## What is a book club edition?

Book clubs licensed popular titles from publishers and printed their own editions, often using the same typesetting but cheaper materials. Members received them at a discount. Because they were printed in large numbers for a different market, collectors treat them as a separate, lesser edition.

## The tell-tale signs

Look for two or more of these:

1. **No price on the dust jacket flap.** Trade editions normally show a price on the front flap; book club jackets usually don't, and some print "Book Club Edition" in the corner where the price would be.
2. **A blind stamp on the back board.** Flip the book over and look at the lower right corner of the back cover (under the jacket). A small indentation such as a dot, circle, square, maple leaf or other shape is a classic book club mark.
3. **Smaller, lighter build.** Book club editions are often slightly shorter and thinner, with thinner boards and cheaper paper that tones faster.
4. **Missing or different ISBN and barcode.** Many book club jackets lack the trade ISBN or barcode.
5. **Different binding materials.** Paper-covered boards where the trade edition used cloth, or simpler stamping on the spine.
6. **Gutter code.** Some book club printings carry a small printed code near the gutter on one of the final pages.

## Why the difference in value?

Collectors want the edition that went to bookshops on publication day, in the materials the publisher chose. Book club editions were produced later, in larger numbers, with cheaper materials and for a different audience. Supply is high and demand from collectors is low, so the market treats them as reading copies.

## The exceptions

There are a few situations where a book club edition can be desirable:

- When the book club edition genuinely appeared **before** the trade edition
- When it is **signed or inscribed** by the author (the signature, not the edition, carries the value)
- Special illustrated editions produced by some clubs that collectors seek in their own right

These are exceptions. Research the specific title before assuming one applies.

## What to do with book club editions

If you're selling, always disclose a book club edition clearly; describing one as a first edition, even unintentionally, leads to returns and disputes. Most are best sold as inexpensive reading copies, included in a bulk lot, or [donated](/guides/where-to-donate-used-books/). If you're buying a first edition, check every listing's photos for the signs above and ask the seller to confirm the jacket price and back board before you pay.

## Quick checklist

- Price on the jacket flap? (Trade editions usually yes)
- Blind stamp on the lower back board? (Book club editions often yes)
- Smaller, thinner, cheaper paper than the trade edition?
- ISBN and barcode on the jacket?

Two or more book club signs, and you can be fairly confident. When the price is significant, compare with a confirmed trade first edition from a reputable seller.
