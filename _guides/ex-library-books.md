An ex-library book is one that was once part of a library collection and later withdrawn and sold. Libraries sell discarded books at sales and to dealers, so ex-library copies are everywhere in the used market. They can be a smart buy, as long as you know what you're getting.

## How to recognise an ex-library book

Look for:

- **Stamps** on the edges, title page or inside covers, often including "Withdrawn" or "Discard"
- **Card pockets** glued inside the covers, and date-due slips
- **Spine labels** with classification numbers
- **Barcodes** and RFID tags
- **Plastic jacket covers** taped or glued to the boards
- **Reinforced or rebound bindings** in heavy library cloth
- **Perforation stamps** embossed through a page

Sellers should always mention ex-library status. If a listing is vague and the price seems too good, ask.

## What ex-library status does to value

For collectors, ex-library markings are a significant defect. A first edition with library stamps, a pocket and a spine label is usually worth a small fraction of a clean copy. Many collectors won't buy them at all.

For readers, the story is different. Library books were often bought in sturdy hardcover editions, reinforced and protected by plastic covers. A withdrawn library copy can be a durable, inexpensive way to own a book you just want to read.

## When an ex-library copy is a good buy

- **You want to read, not collect.** Ex-library copies are often among the cheapest available.
- **The book is scarce.** For some out-of-print scholarly or specialist titles, most surviving copies passed through libraries. An ex-library copy may be the only realistic option.
- **Library bindings appeal.** Some readers like the durability of buckram-bound copies.

## When to avoid them

- **You're building a collection** where condition and originality matter.
- **The listing doesn't disclose markings** and the seller won't answer questions.
- **The book was rebound** and has lost its original covers or jacket, if originality matters to you.

## Selling ex-library books

State ex-library status clearly in the title or first line of the condition note, and list the markings. Grade the rest of the book honestly; ex-library books are commonly graded no higher than Good by collectors, whatever their physical condition. Buyback services and some marketplaces have specific rules for them. Use our [Condition Grader](/tools/condition-grader/) to write an accurate note.

## Don't try to "clean" the markings

Removing stickers, pockets and stamps rarely improves value and often damages the book. A book with traces of removed labels is still ex-library, and buyers may see the removal as concealment. Leave the markings and describe them.
