Hyphens and spaces are ignored. An ISBN-10 may end in X, which stands for 10.

ISBN-10 vs ISBN-13

The International Standard Book Number identifies a specific edition and format of a book. Ten-digit ISBNs were used from 1970; since January 2007 all new ISBNs are 13 digits. Every ISBN-10 has an ISBN-13 equivalent made by adding the prefix 978 and recalculating the last digit. ISBNs beginning with 979 have no ISBN-10 version.

How the check digit works

ISBN-10: multiply the digits by 10, 9, 8… down to 1 and add them up. The total must divide evenly by 11. If the check value works out to 10, it is written as X.

ISBN-13: multiply the digits alternately by 1 and 3 and add them up. The total must divide evenly by 10.

A failed check usually means a typo or a transposed pair of digits — this tool shows the digit the number should end with so you can spot the mistake. Learn more in ISBNs explained.
