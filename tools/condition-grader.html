The standard grading scale

GradeWhat it means
As NewUnread and flawless, as issued. Rare for anything but recent books.
Fine (F)Close to As New; no defects worth mentioning.
Near Fine (NF)Approaching Fine with one or two very small flaws.
Very Good (VG)Used but well cared for; some wear, no tears or major defects; flaws described.
Good (G)An average, complete used copy with wear and possibly marks. Despite the name, this is the middle of the scale.
FairWorn and possibly damaged but complete; a reading copy.
PoorHeavily damaged or incomplete. Usually only worth it for scarce texts.

Dust jackets are graded separately, written as book grade / jacket grade (for example, “Very Good / Good”). Faults such as ex-library status, book club editions, price-clipped jackets and remainder marks are always mentioned in the description, whatever the grade.

Grading is not an exact science and sellers differ. The golden rule: grade conservatively and describe every flaw. Buyers forgive a modest grade; they return books that were oversold. Learn more in Book condition grades explained.
